Curriculum Area 3: Movement and Music

Introduction and Importance · Creativity theories · Resources · Learning experiences · Reflection

Introduction and Importance

Music and movement promote creativity as they make children express themselves physically, rhythmically, and emotionally. By dancing and playing rhythm games as well as sound discovery, the children can learn how to read between the lines in ideas that are not represented through verbal language patterns. Gross motor development and body awareness are promoted with the help of movement, whereas auditory discrimination, memory, and emotional expression are promoted with the help of music. The EYLF Outcome 5 focuses on the expression, which takes place in various forms, such as movement and sound (AGDE, 2022). The movement and music allow those children to risk and improvise, as there is a creative opportunity to collaborate. All these expressional forms enable children to convert ideas into action, thereby provoking imagination, building self-confidence and encouraging holistic development during early childhood.

Creativity Theories and Perspectives

Movement and music correlate to the bodily-kinaesthetic and musical intelligences of Gardner. The sociocultural theory by Vygotsky places rhythmic play as a situation of mutual meaning-making. The latter is considered improvisational creativity (Sawyer, 2023), in which a child works in collaboration to create movement or rhythmic response patterns. A study of neuroscience indicates that with rhythmic motion, executive operations and emotional control are strengthened, which promotes flexible creative thought (Kleibeuker et al., 2016). The divergent thinking theory also finds its application as movement demands children to produce different physical responses and sounds in a creative manner. Music encourages new experimentation of tempo, pitch, and rhythm and is one in which children are able to express an idea in different, creative ways.

Resources, Materials, and Digital Technologies

Open-ended movement can be inspired by the use of scarves, ribbons, drums, shakers, xylophones, floor spots, hoops, and parachutes by teachers. Multimodal expression and rhythmic creativity are promoted with the help of digital tools such as GoNoodle, Just Dance Kids and Chrome Music Lab. Shapes, shadows, or colours projected by projectors contribute to arousing imaginative motions. The audio recorders enable the children to reflect on their performance, thus aiding metacognition. The inclusion can be achieved by the provision of sensory-friendly props. The use of physical props along with digital means would increase the possibilities of creativity and the development of rhythm, tempo, space, and imaginary body performance in children.

Learning Experiences

0–2 years
  • Baby Rhythm Shake – Infants play with soft rattles as educators model the rhythms slowly/fast, which develops auditory exploration and creative responsiveness (AGDE, 2022).
  • Scarf Peek-a-Boo Dance – Baby movements scarf with the help of the carer; they explore the possibility of the amateur use of sensory creativity and cause-and-effect (Promoting Creativity, 2023).
2–3 years
  • Animal Movement Music Play – Children dance as animals according to musical beats, as it helps in promoting imaginary physical expression (García-González et al., 2025).
  • Drum Call-and-Response – The children repeat the rhythmic patterns, which educators make, and learn to listen and improvise (Ghanamah, 2025).
3–5 years
  • Make-Your-Own Dance Sequence – Children compose a simple dance sequence with colourful scarves, and it promotes their ability to compose a movement and feel confident about it (AGDE, 2022).
  • Sound Story Movement – The children would designate movements to a range of sounds and dramatise a sound story (Sawyer, 2023).
6–8 years
  • Group Rhythm Band – Small groups can compose original rhythmic pieces with the help of percussion instruments that will help them to cooperate (Kleibeuker et al., 2016).
  • Digital Choreography (Chrome Music Lab) – Students create simple digital music and choreograph movements to match (Creative Approaches, 2025).

Reflection and Evaluation – Movement and Music

The enactments of movement and music were effective in reaching the minds of the children physically, emotionally, and creatively. Another area that was quite successful was the responsiveness of the children towards rhythm-based cues. They were energetic, eager and eager to experiment and make individual choices of tempo and movement. The collaboration with peers was another positive factor; in particular, children could have the rhythm patterns together, improvise some patterns and share their ideas without feeling intimidated. Such results are consistent with studies that reveal rhythmic play increases executive functioning, improvisation, and social engagement experiences (Kleibeuker et al., 2016).

Nevertheless, there are two aspects which can be enhanced. To begin with, the transitions between activities were at times hasty, and children were not given enough time to reflect and verbalise or showcase their creativity in thinking. Second, abstract digital music patterns needed more scaffolds to read while participating in the activity at Chrome Music Lab.

In a repeat of these experiences, I would make this process slower, with micro-reflection segments interspersed with prompts like, 'Show me your favourite movement,' and 'How did this sound make you feel?' etc. Additional visual cues (e.g., icons contrasting tempo or volume) that would also facilitate children with greater needs would also be introduced by me. These changes would contribute to better metacognitive knowledge, creative agency among children, and their effectiveness as a whole to promote creative movement and expression.

Curriculum Area 10: Integrated Curriculum (STEM, STEAM, STREAM & STEMIE)

Introduction · Theories · Resources · Learning experiences · Reflection

Introduction and Importance

Both STEM, STEAM, STREAM and STEMIE are integrated curriculum models that encourage creativity through combining science, technology, engineering, mathematics, and the arts, as well as inclusive education. These interdisciplinary models enable children to explore issues in the real world and experiment and elaborate on ideas in various forms. EYLF v. 2 focuses on inquiry, connection-making, collaboration and creative problem-solving (AGDE, 2022). Integrated learning also promotes questioning, designing, testing, reflecting and innovating amongst children. Children integrate an artistic expression and a scientific line of thought through STEAM and STREAM. STEMO facilitates reaching everyone through modification of engineering and problem-solving for the children with varying abilities. These methods enhance innovation by making the process complex and meaningful.

Creativity Theories and Perspectives

Integrated curriculum is a representation of constructivist and sociocultural philosophies in which children develop knowledge through inquiry, play and cooperation. The collaborative imagination can manifest itself in Sawyer when children design solutions together and rephrase their thoughts. Further studies on STEM creativities reveal that problem-based learning significantly boosts divergent thinking (García-González et al., 2025). According to Quigley et al. (2020), a strong way of creative design is to consider STEAM because it links the imagination to analytical thinking. The Playworlds study of Fleer reveals that a combination of fiction and inquiry is more helpful in creative scientific explorations. These perspectives have been useful in reinforcing the view that integrated learning is an enriching learning platform that fosters imagination, innovation and critical thinking.

Resources, Materials & Digital Technologies

The teachers will be able to mix materials of all learning domains: recyclables, building kits, coding robots, art supplies, natural loose parts, measuring instruments, ramps, sensorial materials, tablets, digital microscopes, and simple machines. Problem cards, challenge trays, and tinkering stations are some of the provocations of STEAM that encourage experimentation. The use of apps such as Tinkercad, ScratchJr, Chrome Music Lab and digital story creators increases the opportunities to be creative. Multimodal options, adaptive tools, and inclusive materials are used to help all learners in STEMIE-related situations.

Learning Experiences

0–2 years
  • Cause-and-Effect Sensory STEAM Tray – Babies learn the cause and effect of light, sound and moving things by being curious about the world around them at an early age (Fleer, 2019).
  • Texture + Sound Exploration – Infants touch soft, textured materials, bells and crinkles, and their senses, as well as initial inquiry thinking, are reinforced (AGDE, 2022).
2–3 years
  • Colour Mixing & Water Flow Play – children combine colours in tubes and observe how water flows and learn about scientific cause-and-effect processes in a creative way (Promoting Creativity, 2023).
  • Mini Builders STEM Tray – Children use blocks and recycled materials to create simple towers and learn about balance, structure, and basic skills of engineering (Ghanamah, 2025).
3–5 years
  • STEAM Bridge Challenge – Children are required to design and build small bridges through creative combination and use of art, engineering processes and mathematical problem-solving skills (Quigley et al., 2020).
  • STREAM Story Engineering – Children make structures of story characters and engage in imaginative telling and working with real-world engineering exploration and problem-solving (Zhao, 2020).
6–8 years
  • STEMIE Inclusive Design Project – Students design inclusive versions of playgrounds by envisioning the elements of creative solution services for diverse abilities with the help of engineering and empathy (García-González et al., 2025).
  • STEAM Digital Design – Students use Tinkercad or ScratchJr to generate digital prototype projects combining creativity, simple coding, and creative digital design (Sawyer, 2023).

Reflection and Evaluation – Integrated Curriculum

The implementations of the integrated curriculum were much in favour of creativity, problem-solving and interdisciplinary thinking. The work was a success in such a way as the STEAM Bridge Challenge, during which children integrated design, art, and engineering ideas. Their discussions and repeated testing with each other were examples of creative thinking in line with Quigley et al. (2020). The STREAM Story ROBA task was another strength – the children related narrative concepts to structure design and showed full integration of imagination.

Nevertheless, not all children could at first relate information across the curriculum areas, implying that there was a necessity for more explicit modelling of the concepts. In digital STEAM tasks, some children were distracted by some non-task features, which suggests that digital scaffolding should be increased.

In the case of repeating such experiences, I would present a visual representation in the form of the STEAM thinking map that would demonstrate how the areas are interrelated. I would also engage in demonstration videos or mini-provocations to engage prior knowledge prior to the start of design by kids. Metacognition should be reinforced by adding reflection circles, in which children provide explanations on how they utilised science, engineering, art or maths in their construction. Such enhancements would enhance comprehensibility of interdisciplinary relationships among children as well as increase their confidence in integrated learning classrooms.
